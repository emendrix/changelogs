# What you may do with this data, and who owns which part of it

This repository holds two layers that answer to different holders, and the distinction matters
more here than it usually does, because one of them is law.

## The quoted texts are the European Union's

Every `before` and `after` text, every provision title and every act title in this repository is
quoted verbatim from a document published by the Publications Office of the European Union. Those
texts are © European Union, and their reuse is governed by
[Commission Decision 2011/833/EU](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec/2011/833/oj) on the reuse of
Commission documents, which permits reuse for commercial and non-commercial purposes provided the
source is acknowledged.

**Only the Official Journal is authentic.** The consolidated versions this data is computed from
are editorial aids produced by the Publications Office and carry no legal value. Where an answer
matters, read the act in the Official Journal.

## The computed layer is CC BY 4.0

Everything this project derived over those texts is licensed
[CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), whose full text is in `LICENSE` beside
this file. That covers:

- which provisions a diff found to have changed between two consolidated versions, and how each
  change was classified;
- the corroboration between the structural diff, the Union's own modification metadata and the
  amending act's instruction prose, including every disagreement recorded as one;
- the counts, the dates read out of the text, the citations and their resolution;
- the prose written about each change, and the record of which model wrote it and when.

Attribute it to **emendrix**, and link <https://emendrix.eu> where a link is practical.

## Two things this data is not

**It is not legal advice.** It is machine-computed from published texts, carries no lawyer's
review, and is engineering assistance only.

**It is not complete, and it says where it is not.** A change the sources disagree about ships
marked as disputed rather than resolved or dropped; a change whose text a signal could not supply
ships saying so. Those are findings, not gaps to read past, and the methodology page at
<https://emendrix.eu/methodology/> publishes the rate of each over this corpus. Anyone citing a
figure from here should cite the date they read it, because the corpus is corrected as defects are
found and the published rates move when it is.

## If you are reusing this

The honest citation names both layers, for example:

> Regulatory change data computed by emendrix (<https://emendrix.eu>), CC BY 4.0. Quoted
> legislative texts © European Union, reused under Commission Decision 2011/833/EU; only the
> Official Journal is authentic.
